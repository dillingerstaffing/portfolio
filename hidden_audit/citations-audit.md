Date: 2026-09-21. Method: read-only. Each post's article body was read from its generated per-post page at `blog/<slug>/index.html`. The lab-code "evidence" link under each post is not a citation and was excluded. A source counts as relied-on only if the post names it explicitly, quotes it, or paraphrases with attribution. A passing mention ("like TLS does") is not reliance. All quotes below are copied verbatim from the post text.

Only one post (`the-most-honest-lie-in-c`) has any citation entries at all. The other 41 posts have zero Sources sections and zero inline citation markers.

## Summary

- Posts audited: 42.
- Posts with zero citation entries: 41 of 42 (everything except `the-most-honest-lie-in-c`).
- Posts that rely on major external sources with no citation at all: 39 of the 41 uncited posts have clear used-but-not-cited sources (named, quoted, or paraphrased with attribution). Two more are borderline: `the-stack-frame-is-optional` states "the ABI's 16-byte alignment rule" without naming which ABI document, and `the-write-that-sleeps` describes xv6 pipe internals without naming any source, so by the strict rule they rely on unnamed sources the reader cannot check.
- The C post (`the-most-honest-lie-in-c`): all 6 cited sources are genuinely used, each with at least one inline anchor and a leaning sentence (note [1], [4], and [5] are used only in the "What the six sources say" summary paragraph, not in the main argument). Cited-but-not-used: none. Used-but-not-cited: the ISO C11 standard (Annex K / memset_s) and the mlock(2) man page.
- Recurring uncited source types, most to least common: RISC-V ISA documents (privileged spec in roughly 20 posts; unprivileged spec and its F/D/M chapters; ELF psABI; assembler manual; Zicond and vector extension specs), xv6 sources (trap.c, trampoline.S, kalloc.c, memlayout.h, vm.c, walk, kernel/riscv.h, kernel/pipe.c), IEEE 754 (3 posts), Linux (kernel docs, fs/pipe.c, overcommit behavior), POSIX (1 post), glibc (1 post), QEMU docs and QEMU source tree (2 posts), man pages (pidfd_getfd, mlock), plus one-offs: the Sail formal model, a Go compiler proposal, RustSBI docs, a Renode issue report, the ELF format, MIT's xv6 locks lab.
- Three worst offenders: `the-brake-pedal-on-the-stopwatch` (named spec section 3.1.12 plus RustSBI docs plus a named third-party bug report, zero citations); `the-bit-that-survives-the-context-switch` (two verbatim spec quotations, zero citations); `the-four-legal-ways-to-see-memory` (spec Table 114 quoted verbatim with a 16-row transcription, zero citations).

## Bottom line

Thirty-nine of forty-two posts lean on major external sources with no citation entries anywhere, and the RISC-V ISA manuals are the dominant uncited source across the whole blog. The C post's six cited sources all check out as genuinely used, but the same post also leans on the ISO C11 standard and the mlock(2) man page without citing them. Citation coverage is therefore not a C-post problem, it is a blog-wide gap: one post cites, forty-one do not, and the fix is extending the existing `sources` data key to the other posts rather than any change to the C post.
